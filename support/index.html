Quick start

  1. Set a Malinois PIN. Four digits, or six. Use a code that is different from your iPhone passcode and your Guided Access passcode.
  2. Set up Guided Access once. It locks the iPhone to Malinois while it is armed. The steps are below, and the arming screen shows them too.
  3. Arm. Tap ARM. Triple-click the side button and tap Start to begin Guided Access, then tap Start countdown and set the iPhone down. (On a new install, the first Start countdown shows a one-time screen first, with how to disarm and, under Guided Access, how to end it: tap I understand.) When the countdown ends, Malinois calibrates for a few seconds and the screen goes dark: it is watching.
  4. Disarm. Press and hold the screen for 5 seconds and enter your PIN. Then triple-click the side button and enter your Guided Access passcode.

Disarming and your PIN

How do I disarm?

  1. Press and hold anywhere on the screen for 5 seconds. Keep your finger down: a short vibration and a brighter screen mean the keypad is up.
  2. Type your PIN. The keypad submits as soon as you have typed it.
  3. If Guided Access is on, triple-click the side button and enter your Guided Access passcode to end it.

A quick tap does not open the keypad, and Malinois records it as someone touching the iPhone.

How do I tell whether anyone touched it while I was away?

  1. Before typing your PIN, look at Armed for above the pad. It should match how long ago you armed.
  2. After disarming, Home's Last armed line should start at the time you armed. If it starts later, your session ended and another began.
  3. Open the Event Log and read every entry since you armed, not just the newest. A disarm you didn't make, Monitoring interrupted or a wrong PIN means someone tried, even if the phone looks untouched.

The screen went dark. Is it broken?

No. While armed, Malinois darkens the screen so the iPhone looks switched off. It is still watching.

If the iPhone is charging, the screen is dimmed rather than black, with a red REC badge, and iOS shows its own camera indicator. Malinois keeps the camera ready while charging, so a photo is taken instantly. The badge shows whenever the camera is on, and it cannot be turned off. On battery the camera stays off until something trips, so the screen is fully black.

You choose this under Settings → Capture → Battery mode:

  • Auto (the default): the camera is ready while charging and off on battery.
  • Battery saver: the camera is off until something trips, so the screen stays fully black. The first photo takes a moment longer.
  • Instant: the camera is always ready, so the badge always shows.

When do I press ✓?

Usually never: the keypad submits as soon as you have typed your PIN. You will see ✓, and need to press it, on your first entry after updating Malinois, and when you try again after a wrong PIN.

I typed the wrong PIN too many times.

After five wrong tries the keypad locks for 30 seconds, and each further wrong try doubles the wait, up to an hour. The right PIN resets it. On the armed screen, every wrong PIN is recorded in the Event Log, and Malinois tries to take a photo.

Why did Malinois ask for my PIN when I tapped ARM?

When your PIN has not been entered for 14 days, Malinois asks for it before arming, so you find out whether you still remember it before Guided Access keeps the phone in Malinois. Disarming, or entering it anywhere else in the app, starts the 14 days again. After updating to version 1.8.1, your first ARM asks once, unless you have entered your PIN in the app since updating.

I have forgotten my PIN.

Malinois never stores your PIN, only a scrambled check of it, so nobody can look it up. To choose a new one:

  1. If Malinois is armed, end Guided Access first: triple-click the side button and enter its passcode.
  2. Delete Malinois and install it again from the App Store.
  3. Malinois says Your Malinois PIN was kept. Tap Forgot PIN?, verify with Face ID, Touch ID or your iPhone passcode, and choose a new PIN.

Deleting Malinois deletes the evidence kept on this iPhone. With Pro, your iCloud copies come back after you tap Verify iCloud access in Settings → iCloud.

Guided Access

Why does Malinois need Guided Access?

Guided Access is an iOS feature that locks the iPhone to one app. While Malinois is armed under it, nobody can go to the Home Screen, switch apps or close Malinois without your Guided Access passcode, as long as Voice Control is off and Face ID and Touch ID are off for ending Guided Access. Holding the buttons to force a restart still ends Guided Access and the watch; Malinois normally records the handling first and, on Pro with iCloud backup on and a connection, sends it.

Without it, the armed iPhone is unlocked behind a dark screen: anyone who picks it up can leave Malinois and use it. Malinois records the touch and tries to take a photo, but it cannot stop them. That is why Malinois requires Guided Access to arm. For a quick test you can skip it for one arm on the arming screen (the very first arm on a new install skips it by itself), and Malinois warns you to stay nearby. To turn the requirement off for good, use Settings → Security → Require Guided Access.

How do I set it up?

  1. In iOS Settings, go to Accessibility → Guided Access and turn it on.
  2. Under Passcode Settings, set a Guided Access passcode that is different from your iPhone passcode, so someone who knows your unlock code cannot end Guided Access. They could still force a restart, which ends the watch, and then unlock the phone with that code. Leave Face ID (or Touch ID) off there as a way to end Guided Access: anyone who knows your iPhone passcode can add their own face or fingerprint and end Guided Access with it.
  3. In Malinois, triple-click the side button (or the Home button). Before you tap Start, tap Options and turn off Side Button (one press would otherwise lock the screen and pause the watch) and Volume Buttons (so nobody can turn the alarm down).
  4. If you use Voice Control, turn it off before you arm: it keeps working under Guided Access and can close apps.

Guided Access says it is unavailable.

This is an iOS 26 bug, not Malinois: after a Guided Access session ends, iOS can refuse to start a new one for a few minutes (“Guided Access is unavailable”). Wait 3 to 5 minutes, or restart the iPhone. It seems to happen more often when Face ID or Touch ID is set as a way to end Guided Access.

Alerts and Apple Watch

How do alerts reach my other devices?

With Pro on the armed device and Cross-device alerts on (Settings → iCloud), a tamper or a disarm alerts your other iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch signed in to the same Apple Account. They come through iCloud, usually within seconds when both devices are online. Tamper alerts are Time Sensitive, so they get through Focus and Sleep; disarm alerts do not.

An alert that arrives late (for example because the armed iPhone was offline) shows when it arrived, not when it happened. The Event Log shows the real time.

Alerts do not arrive on my other device.

  1. On the armed device, open Settings → iCloud. Cross-device alerts should be on, and iCloud access verified (tap Verify iCloud access if it is shown).
  2. On the other device, open Malinois once and allow notifications. Its Settings → iCloud shows whether Notifications on this device are on.
  3. Check that both devices use the same Apple Account and are online.
  4. Check that iCloud is on for Malinois in iOS Settings, under your name → iCloud.
  5. A Focus can hold a disarm alert. Tamper alerts get through unless Time Sensitive notifications are turned off for Malinois.

Apple Watch

Malinois on Apple Watch shows tamper and disarm alerts on your wrist, including those from the iPhone it is paired with (Pro, with Cross-device alerts on). Open Malinois on the watch once: it signs up, asks to show alerts, and its screen says whether alerts will arrive.

When the watch is near its iPhone, the iPhone sends its own alerts straight to the watch. watchOS decides when to wake the watch app for them, so they can take a little while; each says when it happened. The same alert usually comes through iCloud as well, a few seconds earlier, and then the watch can tap twice; it keeps one of them in its list, the copy with the time. When the iPhone's copy comes first, iCloud's comes quietly. Whether the watch taps or sounds follows its notification settings. Alerts more than an hour old, for example after the watch was off, arrive as one summary instead of one by one. When the watch is away from its iPhone, alerts come through iCloud, usually within seconds, and without the time. The watch shows only the alert text (with its time, for its own iPhone's alerts) or a summary's count, never photos or details.

Evidence, iCloud and privacy

Where does my evidence go? Can you see it?

There is no Malinois server, and I cannot see any of it. Evidence stays on your iPhone, and is included in the iPhone’s own backups when you use them (iCloud Backup, or a backup made with a computer); Malinois’s iCloud backup switch does not change that. With Pro, once you have verified iCloud access, it is also saved to your own private iCloud, so what has uploaded is still there if the iPhone is switched off or lost. See the privacy policy.

You do not have to take my word for it: the app’s source code is public, so anyone can check that there is no server to send anything to. Read the source.

What is “Verify iCloud access”?

Before a newly installed copy of Malinois uploads anything, you confirm with Face ID, Touch ID or your iPhone passcode that it is your iPhone (Settings → iCloud → Verify iCloud access). This stops someone who reinstalls Malinois on your iPhone from reaching your iCloud evidence, unless they can also pass Face ID, Touch ID or your iPhone passcode. Until you verify, detection and capture work as usual, and what Malinois records stays on the iPhone, even after you verify: verify before you rely on iCloud. While Guided Access is on, the button is greyed out: iOS holds Face ID and passcode prompts until Guided Access ends.

How do I stop uploads to iCloud?

Turn off iCloud backup in Settings → iCloud; it asks for Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. Nothing new uploads (a file already being sent finishes), and events recorded while it is off stay on the iPhone for good, even after you turn it back on. Your log shows when it was turned off, and that entry is also sent to iCloud, so your other devices running Malinois 1.7 or later show it too. While it is off, your other devices get no alerts from this iPhone through iCloud; your paired Apple Watch still gets them directly. Turning off Cross-device alerts stops the alerts, not the uploads. Turning Malinois off in iOS Settings, under your name → iCloud, also stops uploads, but events recorded meanwhile upload once it is back on.

Can evidence be deleted?

Not one entry at a time: the Event Log has no way to delete a single entry, not even with your PIN. It keeps your recent events and retires the oldest automatically. Deleting Malinois deletes everything stored on the iPhone. Copies in your iCloud are yours to manage in iOS Settings, under your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage. But each time Malinois starts, it checks that its newest uploads are still there; if most are gone, it says so on Home and uploads again what the iPhone recorded and still holds (while iCloud backup is off, they wait until you turn it back on). To keep them deleted, leave iCloud backup off on the iPhones that recorded them, or delete Malinois from them. Malinois also removes older full-size photos and clips from your iCloud by its iCloud Photo & Video Storage setting (after 12 months unless you change it), keeping each event's details, and never anything newer than 30 days. The setting covers the photos and clips from all your devices: if they are set differently, the shortest setting applies to all of them.

How do I share a photo or clip?

Open the event in the Event Log and tap the share button at the top right.

Troubleshooting

It went off when nothing happened.

Lower the sensitivity for the tripwire that fired (Settings → Sensitivity), or turn it off (Settings → Tripwires). A surface that vibrates is the usual cause for Device motion. Sound is the most likely to go off by mistake, which is why it is off by default. Test sensors on the main screen (behind your PIN) shows each tripwire reacting live, without recording anything.

The alarm is not loud enough.

No app can control the volume buttons, so the alarm plays at your media volume. Turn the volume up before you arm, and in Guided Access Options turn off Volume Buttons so nobody can turn it down. By default the alarm starts quiet for 10 seconds and then rises over the next 10, so you can disarm your own iPhone without a blast. To switch that off, turn off Start quiet, then ramp up under Settings → Tripwire Response.

Face ID unlock turned itself off.

Opening the Event Log with Face ID or Touch ID (off by default; Settings → Security) is tied to the faces or fingers enrolled on your iPhone when you turn it on. If one is added or removed afterwards, Malinois turns the unlock off before offering it, and the PIN pad says so: someone who knows your iPhone passcode could otherwise add their own face or finger. Enter your PIN; to use Face ID again, turn the switch on again in Settings. Updating to 1.8.1 with the switch on asks you to turn it on again once, too.

Why is the Vision tripwire off on battery?

Vision watches the camera’s view for movement, so it needs the camera running, which costs battery. Malinois keeps the camera running only while charging (Battery mode Auto) or always (Instant). On battery with Auto, Vision is off and the arming screen says so; your other tripwires work as normal.

Which camera does Malinois use?

The front camera by default, for an iPhone lying face up. With the Vision tripwire on (Pro), you can choose Rear, or Auto (rear when the iPhone is face down), for an iPhone lying face down and watching the room. Both (Pro) records the front and rear cameras at once; a clip from the two at once has no sound (when they can’t record together, the front camera records alone, with sound if Record audio in clips is on). Choose under Settings → Capture → Camera.

Malinois Pro

What does Pro include?

iCloud backup of your evidence, alerts to your other devices and Apple Watch, both cameras at once, longer clips, and the Sound and Vision tripwires. Detection, capture, the Event Log and the alarm are free, and always will be.

Early Access: while it lasts, Pro is free and permanent for every install. If Pro becomes a paid unlock later, that will apply to new installs only, which will get a 30-day trial. You can still buy Pro now to support the project; it adds nothing you do not already have.

I bought Pro but it is not showing.

In Malinois, open Settings, tap the Pro row, then tap Restore. Make sure you are signed in to the same Apple Account you bought it with.

A note on responsible use

Malinois is for protecting your own device. Because it records whoever handles it, please use it lawfully where you live. It is not a tool for monitoring other people.
