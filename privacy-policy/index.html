Privacy Policy — Malinois

Effective date: 23 October 2026
Developer: John Compton
Contact: support@comptonemail.com

Malinois (“the app”) is a personal tamper-detection tool that runs on your own iPhone. This policy explains what the app accesses, what it records, and where that information goes.

The short version: the developer collects nothing, operates no server, and has no access to anything the app records.

What the app accesses

  • Camera — to capture a photo or short video clip as evidence when your armed device is tampered with. While the device is charging (or always, in the Instant camera mode), the camera session may be held ready so that capture is immediate rather than losing the first seconds to starting the camera. Whenever a camera session is live the app shows an on-screen recording indicator that cannot be disabled. Nothing is recorded or stored while the camera is merely held ready — images are written only when a tripwire fires, or, with the signal-loss response on (the default), when the armed phone has been without the connection it had at arming for 30 seconds and the Motion tripwire has not tripped in the last 10 seconds; an ordinary outage can set this off too.

    If you turn on the optional Vision tripwire, the camera is also used to detect, not only to record: while the camera is held ready, the app compares successive frames in memory, a few times a second, to notice movement in the camera’s view. Those frames are never written to storage, never leave the device, and are discarded as soon as they are compared. As with every other tripwire, an image is saved only once something trips. This tripwire is off unless you switch it on.
  • Microphone — only if you turn on the Sound tripwire or Record audio in clips; both are off by default, and the app asks for microphone access at that moment, not at launch. It listens for sudden sounds nearby as a tamper tripwire, and records audio in an evidence clip. (A clip recorded from both cameras at once, with the camera set to “Both”, has no sound: recording from two cameras leaves no room for the microphone. The app says so in Settings and again before arming. When the two cameras can’t record together, the clip comes from the front camera alone and follows Record audio in clips.)
  • Motion sensors (accelerometer/gyroscope) — to detect the device being moved or picked up. This is on-device sensor access and requires no permission prompt.
  • Notifications — so your other Apple devices can be alerted when a tamper is recorded.

The app does not read your photo library, and does not access your location, contacts, calendars, health data, or any other personal information. If you share an event’s photo or clip and choose Save Image or Save Video, iOS asks the first time whether Malinois may add to your photo library; only what you chose is added.

What the app records, and when

When a tamper is detected on your armed device, the app saves:

  • a photo or short video/audio clip captured by the device’s own cameras and microphone, and
  • event metadata (such as the timestamp, which sensor triggered and its readings, and a device label you optionally set).

It also keeps status entries, armed or not, such as arming and disarming, changes to protection settings, and iCloud backup turned off or on; on Pro with iCloud backup on, these go to your iCloud too. While it is armed, with Pro and iCloud backup on, it sends a heartbeat to your iCloud every five minutes with the battery level, whether the device is charging and its thermal state, so your other devices can show when it was last heard from.

Where that information goes

  • On your device. Evidence is stored locally, encrypted at rest using iOS Data Protection.
  • In your device’s own backups. The evidence on your device is included in its iCloud Backup, when that is on, and in any backup you make with a computer, on the free tier too. The app’s iCloud backup switch does not change that; iCloud Backup is turned on or off in iOS Settings → your name → iCloud → iCloud Backup. These backups are governed by Apple’s Privacy Policy (iCloud Backup is end-to-end encrypted only if you have turned on Apple’s Advanced Data Protection), and the developer cannot access them.
  • In your own private iCloud (optional). With Pro, once you have verified iCloud access in the app, evidence is also saved to your private iCloud database (Apple CloudKit private database) so it survives the device being switched off. This is your personal iCloud storage, governed by Apple’s Privacy Policy. The developer cannot access it. To stop these iCloud copies, turn off iCloud backup in the app’s Settings → iCloud, which asks for Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. From then on one record still uploads: the status entry saying backup was turned off, sent (and retried) until it reaches your iCloud, so your iCloud log shows when backup stopped. Nothing else new is uploaded: an upload already under way stops at its next step, though what iCloud has already received stays there, and events recorded while backup is off stay on the device for good, even after you turn it back on. Turning Malinois off in the iOS Settings app, under your name → iCloud, also stops uploads; events recorded meanwhile stay on the device and are uploaded once it is back on. The app’s Cross-device alerts setting turns off the alerts to your other devices, not these copies.
  • To your other devices (optional). If enabled, a “tamper detected” alert — and, when the app is disarmed, a “disarmed” alert — is delivered to your other Apple devices signed into the same iCloud account, via Apple’s push infrastructure.
  • To your paired Apple Watch (optional). With Pro and Cross-device alerts on, your iPhone also sends its own tamper and disarm alerts straight to its paired Apple Watch, over Apple’s WatchConnectivity, also while iCloud backup is off: the kind of alert, the event’s identifier and its time, never a photo, a clip or the event log. The watch keeps the identifiers and times of a limited number of recent alerts, and a count of older ones it has summed up, so that a second copy of an alert it still remembers is not shown or counted again. Turning off Cross-device alerts stops this.

There is no developer-operated server, database, or analytics endpoint. The developer receives no copy of your evidence, metadata, or usage.

Data the developer collects

None. The app contains no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party SDKs. No data is collected by or transmitted to the developer, and nothing is used to track you across apps or websites.

Verifying this

You do not have to take the above on trust. The app’s source code is published at github.com/john-comptonemail-com/malinois, where the absence of any server, analytics endpoint, or third-party SDK can be checked directly.

Data retention and deletion

  • Evidence is retained locally up to the app’s storage limits, after which the oldest items are pruned automatically. You can review events in the app’s Event Log; there is no way to delete a single event.
  • Deleting the app removes all locally stored evidence and settings, except your Malinois PIN (with its count of wrong attempts and the time it was last entered correctly), the start date of your Pro trial, your Early Access membership and, if you turned on Face ID or Touch ID unlock, a marker of the faces or fingers enrolled at the time, which holds no biometric data. These stay in the device’s Keychain, so a reinstall can’t bypass the PIN or start a new trial; when iCloud Keychain is on, the membership also syncs through it, so it follows you to a new iPhone.
  • Evidence in your private iCloud is yours to manage and delete through your iCloud storage. Each time the app starts, though, it checks that its newest uploads are still there; if most are gone, it says so on Home and uploads again what it recorded and still holds (while the app’s iCloud backup is off, they wait until you turn it back on). To keep evidence deleted from iCloud, leave iCloud backup off on the devices that recorded it, or delete Malinois from them, which also deletes the evidence they hold.
  • The app also removes older full-size photos and clips from your iCloud by its iCloud Photo & Video Storage setting (after 12 months unless you choose otherwise: 1, 3 or 6 months, manual only, or keep forever). It keeps each event’s details and thumbnail and the arm and disarm records, and never deletes anything newer than 30 days. When the setting is a number of months, it also removes this iPhone’s older heartbeats the same way, keeping each armed session’s latest one, which your other devices show as “Last heard from”. Free up iCloud space… in Settings removes full-size photos and clips only. Both act on the photos and clips from all your devices on the iCloud account, not only this iPhone’s: if your devices are set differently, the shortest setting applies to all of them.

Recording other people — your responsibility

Because the app records whoever interacts with your device when it is armed, captured evidence may include images or audio of other individuals. You are responsible for using the app lawfully in your jurisdiction, including any laws that govern recording other people. Use it to protect your own device — not to surveil others.

Children

The app is not directed to children and does not knowingly collect information from children.
