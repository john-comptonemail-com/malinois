## 1. For the owner, in plain English

**Overall assessment.** This is an unusually well-defended codebase. The load-bearing promises held up under adversarial reading far more often than they broke: I could not find a way to disarm without the PIN, to get evidence to the wrong iCloud account, to make a stale cloud copy damage first-hand evidence, to make a download delete older photos, to walk the 30-day cloud-retention floor, to splice or forge an evidence ZIP that the Mac verifier passes, or to reach a PIN-gated screen without the PIN. The journal/retention/set-aside machinery, the evidence-signing chain and the verifier are engineered to a standard I rarely see, and the 1.13 features match their documentation everywhere I checked (the welcome-screen gate, the Vision reference still — signed, shown, carried in the ZIP, and genuinely never uploaded —, the download-fits rule, the per-pull iCloud outcomes, the other-devices status budget, the watch's write-down of relayed alerts, and the "Free up iCloud space" entry). The problems below are real but secondary: one robustness hole in the capture pipeline whose trigger is an iOS corner, a composition of two documented evidence-signing concessions that together let a forged "retired" record pass unremarked, a handful of detector-quality and noise gaps an attacker can lean on, and one now-inaccurate permission string.

**The most serious problems (7):**

1. **A capture that gets stuck can wedge the camera for the rest of the app's life — across disarm and re-arm.** The capture pipeline keeps a single "attempt" slot, and the only code that frees it runs when the camera call returns. The camera's photo path has no timeout of its own and implements only the "photo finished processing" callback — Apple only guarantees the *final* callback (`didFinishCaptureFor`), which is not implemented, and the pipeline's own timeout handler shuts the camera session down while the photo is still pending. If iOS then never delivers the photo callback (an interrupted, invalidated capture — the interruption class the app itself designs for), the slot is never freed: every later capture fails with "the camera is still recovering", until the app is relaunched. Detection, the siren and uploads keep working, and each failed event records its reason, so this is not silent — but a night's evidence can be photo-less. Small, safe fix; take it before submission if re-gating is cheap, otherwise first in 1.13.1. (glm-5.3-1)

2. **A stranger's phone can erase one of your signed records from a bundle's verifiable history, and the verifier will present it as a routine size-limit removal.** The verifier accepts "these counters were retired by the log's size limit" statements not only from a key itself but from any key that follows it — with no cap and no attribution in the report. SECURITY.md concedes the ingredients separately (a foreign phone can make its key follow yours after an edited backup; an edited-backup restorer can make the limit "remove" a chosen record on their own phone), but not the composition: someone needs only one genuine bundle from you and their own iPhone to produce a new bundle in which your incriminating record reads as "removed by the size limit", with no missing counter and no problem line. The fix is wording, not cryptography: print follower-authored retirements as such, and add one line to SECURITY.md's cannot-show list. (glm-5.3-2)

3. **If iCloud holds many records this version cannot decode, every pull walks all of them — every launch, every silent push, every pull-to-refresh.** The fetch loop bounds itself by *decoded* records only, so unreadable records (a newer TestFlight build's records, then a downgrade — a supported TestFlight scenario — or garbage from an authorized client) make each pull minutes long, repeated forever until the next update. Home honestly says "couldn't read all of your event log" the whole time; the cost is battery, latency and an unusable restore. One-line class of fix: bound *examined* records too. (glm-5.3-3)

4. **Each time the microphone restarts itself mid-session, the first readings it reports are the −160 floor, and the Sound tripwire's bar sinks a few dB for some seconds — making the unchanged room loud enough to trip.** This is the same failure class the project already fixed for the mic-is-down case; the just-restarted case slips past that guard because the recorder *is* recording again. Repeated calls or Bluetooth churn can keep the bar bouncing low and mint false Sound events — in Siren mode, false alarms in an empty room, the exact owner-wear-down the docs promise cannot happen. (Note: it makes the tripwire *more* sensitive, not less.) (glm-5.3-4)

5. **A jammer that cycles can mint a full-volume siren and a "Signal loss" incident roughly every 30 seconds, with no per-session cap.** The stand-down-after-reconnect re-arm is a deliberate, documented decision (so a brief jam can't burn the one-shot), but unlike Sound outages (≤3 recorded per session) and floods (coalesced), blackout records are unbounded: ~2 sirens, records, frame attempts and pushes to your other devices per minute, for as long as the attacker cares — desensitizing you and filling the incident budget so older genuine evidence is evicted. (glm-5.3-5)

6. **If iOS starts an audio interruption but never posts its "ended" notice — a known iOS behaviour in some interruption classes — the siren stays silent for the rest of its window with no notice.** The design resumes at full volume *when the interruption ends*; nothing re-invokes it periodically while it should be sounding. The next trip revives it, and Siren mode is opt-in, but a thief whose interruption swallows the "ended" notice has silenced the deterrent without the PIN. Needs a device probe to confirm; the fix is a small repeating watchdog. (glm-5.3-6)

7. **The camera permission string still says the Vision tripwire watches "in memory only" — since 1.13 that is no longer the whole truth.** A slow Vision trip now writes the view it compared against to disk with the event. The watching itself is still in memory and the still is ordinary evidence, but this is the one string a privacy reviewer reads, it is now inaccurate in the storage direction, and the fix is one line that ships with this binary. Fix before submission. (glm-5.3-7)

**Verdict: would I let 1.13 (89) go to the App Store?** **Yes.** I found no Critical defect: no disarm without the PIN, no evidence loss or forgery *on the phone* without notice, no evidence to the wrong account, no near-certain App Review rejection. What must be fixed **before submission** is small: the camera usage string (one line, user-facing, ships with this binary), and — while the store pages and public README are being touched anyway for the planned publication — the two stale evidence-ZIP wording items (SECURITY.md's ZIP contents list and the README's iCloud-restore omission, both text-only). The capture-slot fix (1) is small and low-risk and I would take it before submission if the release gate can be re-run cheaply; if not, it belongs at the top of **1.13.1**, together with the pull bound (3), the audio restart guard (4), the jamming record cap (5), and — after the device probe — the siren watchdog (6). The verifier attribution change (2) is a repository/verifier fix that can follow in 1.13.1; it does not block the binary. Everything else in the findings table is 1.13.1 or later, and the monetization test debt is owed before the build that closes Early-Access, not before this one.

## 2. The security model

**As the project states it.** Malinois protects *the fact and evidence of tampering* on an unattended, unlocked iPhone: detection (motion/power/proximity/touch free; sound/vision with Pro), on-device capture, a PIN-gated append-only-style log with retention budgets, an optional deterrent, and — with Pro — a two-shot iCloud exfiltration whose tiny "fact" record races a power-off. Assumptions: the owner sets a distinct Malinois PIN; Guided Access is the designed configuration (required by default, with the first arm auto-lifted); the device has a passcode; the OS is not compromised. Stated non-goals: a forced restart, thermal/battery shutdown, a compromised/jailbroken OS, deletion of the app from the unlocked home screen, wholesale iCloud deletion through Apple's own screens, and an authorized client's ability to alter records. Credentials are cleanly separated (Malinois PIN / GA passcode / biometrics for two read surfaces only / device-owner auth for recovery, verification and the account-wide switches / the iCloud account), and SECURITY.md's tiering is unusually honest about what each tier wins.

**Adversary 1 — a thief with the armed phone, no PIN.** What Malinois detects: the tripwire set is sound and matches its documented thresholds; the touch path cannot be switched off; the hold-to-reveal is the only pad path; every wrong PIN is photographed and counted; latches re-arm; trips during a capture fold into the open event; disarm records and heartbeats escape when Pro and network allow. What it prevents: leaving the app (GA), soft power-off (GA config), PIN guessing (uptime-clock lockout, 30 s→1 h, count survives restarts; verified end-to-end). What it openly does not do: force-restart, sensor thresholds, deletion via the home screen — all stated. **Gaps found:** the jamming-cycle record/siren mint (glm-5.3-5) and the audio-restart false-alarm window (glm-5.3-4) are the two a thief can *lean on*; the siren-interruption gap (glm-5.3-6) and the capture wedge (glm-5.3-1, if iOS drops the delegate) are the two an unlucky or prepared thief can *land in*; proximity has no re-seed after a suspension (glm-5.3-16), and a stalled Vision tap re-anchors silently after 5 s with no record (glm-5.3-17) — the latter within SECURITY.md's blanket "other sensor failures produce neither an event nor a warning" admission.

**Adversary 2 — someone who knows the owner.** PIN guessing on the phone is impractical (verified: the service owns both halves of the rate-limit contract; auto-submit gives no free guesses; a wrong confirmed-length cannot lock the owner out). "Forgot PIN?" correctly demands device-owner auth and fails closed; reinstall leaves the PIN in the Keychain and routes to continuity, not to open setup; the first-PIN "hijack" on a genuinely PIN-less install exists (pre-1.13 behaviour, informational here). **Gap:** the welcome gate's "sign of an earlier install" set misses two surviving markers plus two off-container artefacts (glm-5.3-9) — a contrived selective-destruction path, not a practical one.

**Adversary 3 — the iCloud account, other devices, the developer.** Uploads are bound to the verified account at birth, re-checked at every step, and invalidated on account change; first-hand evidence is never replaced by a mirror; monotonic fields cannot be walked back; downloaded media joins a signed record only under its fingerprint; wholesale cloud deletion is detected at the next launch and first-hand events requeue; made-up records are clamped, bounded and labelled. **Gaps:** the follower-key retirement composition (glm-5.3-2) is the one place where conceded capabilities compose into an *unattributed* erasure in a recipient's check; disarm alerts have no burst quieting (glm-5.3-11), so a compromised client can flood ordinary-level alerts without the tamper burst cap; an unverified Pro install answers `.failed` to every push wake (glm-5.3-15), burning battery until the owner verifies.

**Adversary 4 — App Review.** The store texts are accurate where I could check them (all What's New 1.13 bullets true of the code; counts within every Apple cap — description at 3,996/4,000; background mode `remote-notification` declared = implemented in both apps; permission strings cover exactly what the app asks for; privacy manifests clean; no third-party code; "spy" long dropped from keywords). The recording-indicator story for 2.5.14 is strong and the reviewer notes answer the questions before they are asked. **Risks:** the camera permission string's "in memory only" (glm-5.3-7) is the one claim a privacy reviewer could now falsify; the screenshots date from 1.8 and 1.13 changed Home/arming visibly — an eyeball before submission is due (I could not view images); the description's 4-character headroom means any late edit needs a recount.

**Adversary 5 — the owner on a bad day.** The downgrade path is the weakest: unreadable records make every pull unbounded (glm-5.3-3). Offline, storage-full, sealed-launch and locked-phone cases are otherwise handled with a care that kept surprising me (deferred loads, owed notes, frozen files never overwritten, best-effort deletes that refuse rather than lose). The set-aside Delete's note-first design holds, including its documented storage-too-full exception.

**Adversary 6 — a shared event's recipient.** The bundle format, the counters, the per-revision attachment digests and the verifier's ZIP gauntlet are strong; I found no splice, no misparse, no crash path in the strict readers, and the 1.13 reference still is signed and byte-checked like a photo. What a pass proves is honestly worded. **Gaps:** the follower-retirement attribution (glm-5.3-2); folder-mode symlink following in the verifier (glm-5.3-24); a quadratic duplicate-key check that lets a crafted attestation stall a check for minutes (glm-5.3-23); a size-then-read TOCTOU and the 4 GiB unpack budget (glm-5.3-25/26).

**Recommendations (quick fixes, not design changes).** In priority order: implement `photoOutput(_:didFinishCaptureFor:error:)` and drain the pending photo waiter there, plus a delayed force-release of the attempt slot (1); attribute follower retirements in the verifier's COUNTERS line and add the one SECURITY.md cannot-show line (2); bound the pull walk on examined records (3); skip floor-level ticks for ~0.3 s after an audio recorder restart, or give `clampedRolling` a floor (4); cap blackout records per armed session the way Sound outages are capped (5); make the alert window's timer re-call `ensureSounding()` while it should sound (6); fix the camera string (7). All are small, all are in safe directions.
